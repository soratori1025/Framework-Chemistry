\begin{abstract}
Descriptor reduction in QSAR is usually a marginal scorecard: rank columns by
their univariate correlation with the endpoint, keep the strongest, prune
correlated pairs. That procedure cannot promote a descriptor whose association
is masked by a correlated partner, cannot see a feature that is informative only
in combination, and never estimates the variability of its own ranking. We
present SIREN, a greedy forward selector whose objective is evaluated
conditional on the columns already chosen: a conditional
mutual-information relevance term, an interaction-information term built from an
orthogonalised product basis, a resampling stability term, and an optional
redundancy penalty. Two mechanisms make the objective usable on real descriptor
matrices: a near-duplicate screen that stops a copied column from consuming a
slot, and a permutation-calibrated synergy seed that makes pure interaction pairs
reachable. We evaluate the selector on planted-structure benchmarks with a known
answer, on five molecular endpoints under nested selection over ten splits, with
linear and random-forest readouts, and under a leakage protocol that quantifies
the optimism of selecting before splitting. SIREN recovers suppressed proxies
and pure interaction pairs that the scorecard misses, matches it where no
conditional structure exists, and we quantify how reproducible each selector's
chosen set is across folds. We report the failure modes, including the
incomplete recovery of interaction-only designs, and release the code and
selection traces.
\end{abstract}
