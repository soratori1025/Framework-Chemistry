\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}

We stated three hypotheses in the Background so that they could fail. The results answer them as follows.

\emph{H1, evidence is usable: partly supported.} Criterion (i) is met: with a Random Forest downstream, the recommended subset had the better mean in all 20 dataset--budget cells, and the 50-draw rank test separated it from random subsets in 16 of them, all twelve regression cells and four of the eight classification cells. Criterion (ii) is not met: the rule ranked fourth of six selectors overall (mean rank 3.65, against 2.20 for Random Forest importance), and the block test, while rejecting equality, does not separate it from the best selector. Criterion (iii) is met: the mean Jaccard overlap of the selected sets across splits was 0.76. The correlation with the target is a weak guide to which descriptors a model will actually use, so the honest description is a stable and inspectable way to generate a compact candidate set, not a claim that the selected descriptors are the important ones.

\emph{H2, the descriptor effect transfers: supported on four of five endpoints, and consistent on the fifth once diagnosed.} Adding 210 descriptors to a graph model changed the error by a similar amount in two independently written implementations on FreeSolv ($+12.1\%$ and $+19.7\%$), Lipophilicity ($+0.1\%$ and $+0.2\%$), BBBP ($+0.2\%$ and $+1.3\%$) and BACE ($-2.1\%$ and $-1.9\%$). ESOL was the exception, and it is instructive: Chemprop's descriptor hybrid was better than graph-only on nine of ten splits and failed on one (test RMSE $2.76$). Retraining that configuration spans $0.75$--$6.12$ across six seeds, and bounding the descriptor inputs collapses the spread to $0.65$--$0.72$, close to the graph-only arm on the same split; the ESOL effect is therefore approximately zero rather than reversed, which is what the framework's own contrast gives ($+0.1\%$). A mean without its per-split record would have produced a confident and wrong statement about chemistry, and the same record is what made the failure diagnosable. The practical consequence is that a descriptor effect should be reported per split, and a descriptor block bounded, before either is interpreted.

\emph{H3, route and role matter but are endpoint-specific: supported.} The best route differed by endpoint: the full 210-descriptor hybrid on ESOL and FreeSolv, the recommender on Lipophilicity, graph-only on BACE, and no distinguishable winner on BBBP. The best predictive role also changed with the endpoint. Crucially, no compact route separated from a random subset of the same size once paired uncertainty was included, so the route is worth testing but should not be sold as a source of large gains on these endpoints.

Taken together, the three answers point to a practical rule for a chemist: test the alternatives on your own splits, keep the per-split record, and treat a mean that hides one divergent run as unfinished. \framework{} exists to make that rule cheap to follow. An experiment is a plain-text configuration file; the five commands behind it read the data, audit it, validate the configuration, fit the declared representations, and write a record containing the configuration hash, split seed, predictions and checkpoint. A colleague or referee can therefore see which choice was made, on which evidence, and what happened when it was changed. The framework does not promise a universally better predictor, and the results here give no reason to expect one.
